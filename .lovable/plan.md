# Homepage repositioning plan

## Strategic diagnosis

The homepage already has a strong visual signature: black canvas, orange accent, tactile bottle sequence, restrained typography, and image-led work. The problem is positioning, not identity.

### What is currently weak

- **The hero describes a creative studio, not the product-launch system.** “Creative Lab” and “Branding, packaging, and storytelling” frame Liten as a set of agency services. The strongest differentiator, sourcing real packaging and coordinating the path to a physical sample, is not stated.
- **The two hero actions have the wrong priority.** “Start your brand” is primary while “Explore packaging” is secondary. This sends visitors into a generic contact path before they understand the most tangible offer.
- **“The three labs” gives Branding, Packaging, and Storytelling equal weight.** That is internally tidy but commercially weak. It omits IDEA and SAMPLE, makes the journey feel modular rather than managed, and hides packaging’s role as the entry point.
- **The packaging preview looks like a portfolio grid.** It has the right location and scale, but only shows names and repeated dummy photography. It does not yet communicate a browsable supplier-backed catalog, selection criteria, customization potential, or the path to requesting a sample.
- **The current packaging page overpromises in places.** Its copy says “high-end mockups,” while product details currently show generic hardcoded specifications such as MOQ and lead time. Those values should not become homepage promises until they are verified per product and supplier.
- **“Collabs” is visually useful but commercially vague.** It shows attractive outputs without explaining the founder’s starting point, what Liten managed, or what reached the shelf. Labels are hover-dependent, so the story is weaker on touch screens.
- **“The Litenby way” repeats an agency belief statement.** It does not explain the operational bridge between packaging suppliers, branding, samples, quantities, and launch support.
- **The conversion path is incomplete.** The homepage links to a general contact form; the existing “request a sample” control on product details is not connected to a request flow. The redesigned homepage should not imply a transaction or confirmed availability that the current architecture cannot support.
- **The opening experience is media-heavy.** The homepage preloads both 35-frame hero sequences and autoloads three lab videos. The redesign should retain the signature bottle interaction while staging noncritical media to protect mobile loading and scrolling.

## Recommended homepage structure

### 1. Navigation: lead with the commercial entry point

- Keep the current compact black navigation and Liten logo.
- Reorder the key path so **Packaging** is first.
- Add a “how it works” anchor on the homepage.
- Rename the main action from **start your brand** to **start a product**.
- Keep Branding, Storytelling, Blog, and Contact available, but visually subordinate to the packaging and product-start paths.

### 2. Hero: sell the finished outcome

Retain the bottle sequence as the signature first impression, but change the message hierarchy:

- Pretitle direction: **PRODUCT LAUNCH SYSTEM** or **FROM IDEA TO SHELF**.
- Headline direction: **turn your idea into a shelf-ready product.**
- Supporting copy direction: Liten helps founders choose real packaging, build the brand around it, coordinate a physical sample, and prepare the product for launch.
- Primary action: **explore packaging**.
- Secondary action: **start your product**.

The value statement and actions should be readable immediately rather than waiting for desktop scroll progress. Bottle motion should support the story, not gate it.

### 3. Product path: IDEA → PACK → BRAND → SAMPLE → LAUNCH

Replace “The three labs” with one clear five-step system.

- **Idea:** define the product, audience, positioning, and practical requirements.
- **Pack:** browse real container directions sourced through Liten’s supplier network.
- **Brand:** build the identity, label system, and packaging artwork around the selected form.
- **Sample:** coordinate a physical sample for review before quantities are produced.
- **Launch:** add campaign, content, and storytelling support where needed.

Visual treatment: one continuous editorial sequence, not five dashboard cards. Use large step numbers, fine rules, close-up material imagery, and concise copy. On desktop it can progress horizontally with restrained scroll emphasis; on mobile it becomes a clear vertical sequence. No essential information should depend on hover.

### 4. Packaging showroom: the dominant commercial section

Move the packaging catalog preview directly after the process and give it substantially more visual weight.

- Rename the section toward **start with the pack** or **the packaging showroom**.
- Explain that these are packaging forms founders can explore as a starting point, with final specifications, availability, minimum quantities, and sampling confirmed during the project.
- Show a curated selection rather than treating all items as equal portfolio tiles.
- Use larger product photography with consistent neutral lighting when final assets arrive.
- Keep useful visible metadata only: container type, material, broad use case, and a clear “view packaging” action.
- Do not show unverified MOQ, lead time, stock, price, or “sample available” claims.
- Preserve the existing hover image swap on pointer devices, but make every item fully understandable without hover on mobile.
- Primary section action: **explore all packaging**.
- Secondary contextual action: **have a pack in mind? start your product**.

The current dummy thumbnails remain temporary. The redesign will establish the correct layout, cropping rules, and information model so real product photography can be dropped in later without redesigning the section again.

### 5. The managed bridge: clarify Liten’s role

Introduce a concise editorial section explaining what Liten actually manages:

- packaging search and supplier coordination
- brand identity and label development around the chosen container
- sample coordination and review
- preparation for quantity production
- optional storytelling and launch support

Copy must distinguish **coordination and sourcing** from manufacturing or filling. Recommended language: “Liten manages the path between your idea, packaging partners, brand system, sample, and launch.” Avoid claims that Liten itself manufactures, formulates, fills, or holds inventory unless those capabilities are later verified.

### 6. Selected launches: turn “Collabs” into evidence

Reframe the current gallery as **from concept to shelf** or **selected launches**.

- Feature three stronger stories before showing a larger gallery.
- Each item should state the starting challenge, the parts Liten handled, and the resulting product expression.
- Use visible labels on mobile and desktop, not hover-only descriptions.
- Keep imagery dominant and editorial. Avoid badges, ratings, marketplace chrome, or SaaS-style case-study cards.
- Do not add performance metrics, client quotes, retailer logos, or production claims without supplied evidence.

### 7. Branding and launch support: supporting capabilities, not equal products

Condense Branding and Storytelling into two visually rich editorial bands after the proof section.

- Position Branding as the system built around the chosen product and packaging.
- Position Storytelling as optional launch support that helps the finished product enter the market clearly.
- Link to the existing dedicated pages for depth.
- Reuse the strongest current videos, but load them only when near the viewport.

### 8. Final conversion section

Replace the broad agency-style close with a product-specific decision:

- Headline direction: **ready to turn the idea into something you can hold?**
- Primary action: **explore packaging**.
- Secondary action: **start your product**.
- Supporting reassurance: founders can begin with an idea, a packaging reference, or an existing product that needs repositioning.

## CTA hierarchy

1. **Explore packaging**: primary discovery action in the hero, showroom, and final section.
2. **Start your product**: primary inquiry action in the navigation and secondary hero action.
3. **View packaging / view project**: item-level actions.
4. **Explore Branding / explore Storytelling / read stories**: supporting pathways.

“Start your brand” should be removed from the homepage because it narrows the offer too early. CTA labels should remain consistent across desktop and mobile.

## Copy direction

- Lead with the founder’s outcome: a real, coherent product ready for the shelf and launch.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs: choose, source, build, sample, review, produce, launch.
- Explain packaging early and repeatedly without calling Liten a marketplace or claiming live inventory.
- Describe Liten as the managed bridge and single creative-operational partner.
- Keep copy concise, confident, and editorial. Avoid agency abstractions such as “exceptional experiences,” “full service,” and “we bring visions to life.”
- Avoid em dashes, in line with the established copy rules.

## Visual and interaction treatment

- Preserve the pure black, white, and Liten orange identity.
- Keep the bottle sequence, but make the commercial message immediately available and reduce the amount of empty scroll before the proposition is understood.
- Replace rounded service-card styling with sharper editorial grids, thin dividers, controlled spacing, and product-photography scale.
- Use orange sparingly for action, sequence markers, and key words, not decoration.
- Keep interactions tactile and purposeful: bottle sequencing, product image swaps, subtle process progression, and concise image reveals.
- Avoid generic dashboard filters on the homepage, oversized pill clusters, decorative glows, floating cards, and marketplace patterns.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid hiding important content behind reveal animation.
- Build mobile as its own reading sequence: outcome, actions, five steps, showroom, proof, support, final action.

## Typography decision

The current source uses **Plus Jakarta Sans** for headings and **Urbanist** for body copy; it does not currently load Syne. To honor the requested Liten identity, implementation should use **Syne as the display/headline face** and retain Urbanist for readable supporting copy, while preserving the lowercase headline behavior. If the mention of Syne was not intended as a font change, this is the one item to revise before implementation.

## Realistic implementation approach

- Recompose the existing homepage route rather than rebuilding the whole site.
- Retain the current navigation, hero sequence, packaging data, project imagery, footer, and dedicated routes where useful.
- Replace “Three Labs” with a five-step process component.
- Rework the existing packaging preview into a reusable showroom component connected to the current packaging route.
- Reframe the existing collab assets as selected launch stories.
- Rewrite and restructure the current About section into the managed-bridge explanation and final conversion block.
- Stage-load the lab videos and avoid loading both desktop and mobile hero sequences when only one is needed.
- Keep sample requests as inquiry-based navigation until a real request workflow is defined. No database, checkout, inventory status, or fulfillment logic is assumed in this homepage phase.

## Validation criteria

- A first-time visitor can explain Liten as “idea to packaging to brand to sample to launch” after the first two sections.
- Packaging is visibly the primary entry point and largest commercial section.
- No copy implies Liten manufactures, fills, stocks, or guarantees supplier terms.
- Every essential label and action works without hover.
- Primary actions are consistent on desktop and mobile.
- The signature bottle experience remains intact, with improved loading discipline and reduced-motion support.
- The homepage feels editorial and tactile, not like an agency service menu, SaaS product, or open marketplace.
